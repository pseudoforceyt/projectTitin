## <center>Video Games and Their Impact on Teens

<p style='text-align: justify;'>
Video games are often frowned upon as a "waste of time" or a "distraction from studies." Isolated headlines about the negative side of gaming strengthen the opinion that games are bad for teenagers. However, this point of view overshadows the many positive aspects of video games. This essay argues that video games should be recognized for their potential to support growth in areas such as thinking skills, learning, relationships, and emotional balance.

One major and most relevant aspect to parents is the improvement of cognitive skills such as memory, decision-making, and spatial reasoning. Strategy and puzzle games, such as Portal and Civilization, help teens think critically and respond quickly to challenges. Studies show that video games can enhance brain functions related to visual attention and mental flexibility (Bavelier and Green).

Contrary to the popular belief that gamers are isolated, many teenagers use video games as a platform to build social connections. Online multiplayer games, such as Valorant, encourage social interaction, particularly valuable in areas where outdoor play spaces are limited. Research shows that online gaming communities can strengthen friendships and provide a sense of belonging, especially for teens who struggle with face-to-face interactions (Kowert et al.).

Video games also serve as emotional outlets. With the high academic and societal pressure that many students face, games offer an escape from daily stress. Casual games, in particular, have been found to reduce anxiety and improve mood (Russoniello et al.). For teens with limited access to mental health support, this kind of stress relief is especially important.
</p>

Daphne Bavelier, a leading cognitive neuroscientist, summarizes this argument best:

> "The term ‘video games’ is far from a single construct and thus has almost no scientific predictive power. One can no more say what the effects of video games are than one can say what the effects of food are." (qtd. in Bavelier)

<p style='text-align: justify;'>
In essence, parents should show their children the good side of video games. They should be aware of both the benefits and risks in order to guide their children while they explore the exciting and empowering world of gaming.
</p>
